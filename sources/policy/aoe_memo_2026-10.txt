                              MEMORANDUM
TO:           Vermont State Board of Education
FROM:         Dr. Erin Davis, Chief Academic Officer, Agency of Education
SUBJECT:      Revised Proposal for Vermont Statewide Graduation Requirements
DATE:         October 20, 2026



Purpose
Vermont’s new statewide graduation requirements will exist to ensure every student is
fully prepared for success in college, career, and civic life. Therefore, we must maintain
high expectations for all students, and we will not lower these expectations based on
current constraints as we await important education transformation decisions. However,
as the state navigates these key funding, governance, and federal timeline decisions,
we recommend building temporary flexibility into the first two graduating classes (2031
and 2032). This approach ensures Vermont achieves full compliance with the
Elementary and Secondary Education Act (ESEA) and gives supervisory unions and
districts (SU/SDs) time to transition smoothly without compromising equal opportunity
for students.

Federal Compliance
Act 73 mandates that the State Board of Education (SBE) adopt uniform statewide
graduation requirements taking effect in the 2027–2028 school year for the Class of
2031 and subsequent classes. Additionally, federal accountability requires the state to
report its graduation rate according to federal definitions of the four-year adjusted cohort
graduation rate, per 20 U.S.C. §7801(23). Under Act 73, these requirements establish a
single, statewide standard for a standard diploma; local supervisory unions and school
districts (SU/SDs) cannot deviate from this standard by requiring either higher or lower
total credits. Vermont will benefit from having consistent practices in our graduation
requirements that align to our federal reporting obligations. Deviations between practice
and data reporting leads to confusion and a heightened risk of error.
Uneven rigor in locally determined graduation requirements—stemming from provisions
such as Section 2120.8 of Vermont's 2025 Education Quality Standards
(EQS)—has placed Vermont out of compliance with federal requirements
as cited in the Vermont Consolidated Performance Review Report 2024
following a monitoring visit from the U.S. Department of Education. The
statewide requirements mandated by Act 73 serve as a key component of Vermont’s
official federal corrective action plan with the U.S. Department of Education. Because
federal law supersedes state law and administrative rule, Vermont must adhere to
federal compliance expectations.
Furthermore, implementing these requirements in Fall 2027 means Vermont will not
achieve full federal compliance until the Class of 2031 graduates. The U.S. Department
of Education has expressed significant concern over the length of this timeline,
reinforcing the urgency of maintaining a firm statewide standard without further delay or
local variation.

Updates to AOE Recommendations
To reconcile these operational realities with federal compliance timelines under ESEA,
and based on important stakeholder input throughout the State Board of Education’s
decision-making process, the Agency proposes a phased implementation model:
Phase 1: Transitional Flexibility (Classes of 2031 and 2032)
The total required credit threshold remains fixed at 22.5 credits. To allow SU/SDs time
to build capacity in newly mandated areas (e.g., World Languages and additional
Math/Health credits), 3.0 required credits in these subjects are temporarily shifted into
the elective category to allow for increased flexibility. This provides districts with a
manageable transition period while maintaining a consistent overall credit standard
across the state to satisfy federal requirements.
Phase 2: Full Implementation (Class of 2033 and Beyond)
Beginning with the Class of 2033, the full recommended distribution across all subject
areas will take effect. Crucially, because these requirements take effect as students in
the classes of 2031 and 2032 enter their junior and sophomore years, students who
wish to satisfy the complete requirements before their graduation will still have full
opportunity to do so.

Conclusion & Next Steps
Establishing a uniform 22.5 credit standard satisfies federal corrective action under
ESEA Section 8101(43) by ending local deviations that create inequitable educational
opportunities across Vermont, while ensuring overall rigor remains firm across all
cohorts in alignment with national benchmarks and flexible pathways under Act 77.
By temporarily shifting 3.0 credits (1.0 Math, 1.0 World Language, and 1.0 PE/Health
adjustment) into open electives for the Classes of 2031 and 2032, this phased plan
gives local districts essential operational flexibility to build staffing capacity without
compromising federal compliance or student access to postsecondary entrance
