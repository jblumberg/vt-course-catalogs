# CCSS-ELA Standards Coverage

The term **coverage** refers to the standards to which students will be exposed through teaching and learning. All CCSS-ELA standards should be covered, to some extent, every year.

Each credit is made up of the same **six standards clusters**, which are composed of one **priority standard** and multiple **contributing standards**.

## Standards Clusters

1.  Reading Literature

2.  Reading Informational Text

3.  Writing

4.  Research

5.  Collaborative Discussion

6.  Presentation

The Language strand of the CCSS is divided among the six standards clusters. Each Common Core Standard for ELA appears as either a priority standard or contributing standard.

## Introductory Credits (Grades 9-10)

All grades 9-10 banded standards would be covered across two introductory credits (generally grades 9 and 10). Priority standards would be covered in each credit. Contributing standards to include in the standards cluster would be selected so that every standard gets covered **at least once** over the course of both credits. *Table 1* shows the six priority standards and contributing standards in each cluster.

# Threshold for Earning an ELA Credit

In order to create equity and consistency across the state, there needs to be a common threshold for earning a credit of ELA. The threshold for earning an ELA credit is the extent to which students need to demonstrate proficiency in the standards included in each cluster in order to earn a credit. Below are two possible models depicting the threshold for earning credit.

## Model A – Required and Recommended Contributing Standards

In Model A, certain contributing standards would be required in order for a student to earn a credit. The rest of the contributing standards in the cluster would be recommended but would not be required as part of the threshold for proficiency.

Each credit would therefore consist of:

- The six priority standards;

- Specific required contributing standards (half in the first grade level in the band, the rest in the second); and

- Any number of recommended contributing standards that aid in the teaching and learning of the standards cluster.

Table 3 below illustrates the division of required and recommended standards for each grade band. Following a model such as this would result in students needing to demonstrate proficiency in around 25 standards per credit.

For example, Table 4 shows the proposed Writing standards cluster for grades 11-12. In order for a student to demonstrate that they can “\[p\]roduce clear and coherent writing in which the development, organization, and style are appropriate to task, purpose, and audience” and meet the priority standard, they would need to write at least one argument, informative/explanatory piece, and narrative (W.1-3). Writing across these three writing types also incorporates W.10. Each writing type includes a list of sub-standards that could help inform the development of the success criteria for that writing type. Additionally, W.5 would be covered by making sure students are planning, drafting, revising, and rewriting and W.6 would be covered if they use technology to help enhance the writing process. W.8 and/or W.9 could be added as success criteria for an informative/explanatory essay or argument, L.3 could be added to a narrative, and L.6 could be applied to any piece of writing. Finally, all writing types could be assessed for conventions, so L.1 and L.2 could easily be incorporated into the success criteria.
