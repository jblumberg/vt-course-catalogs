# Purpose

The Agency of Education, in conjunction with support from the State Board of Education, is working to determine statewide graduation requirements that are to be met by *all students* in all supervisory unions and school districts across the state. Act 73 directs the State Board of Education (SBE) to adopt standards for statewide graduation requirements based on SBE-approved standards and recommendations from the Agency of Education, taking effect beginning in the 2027–2028 school year for the graduating class of 2031 and every graduating class thereafter. There are many steps to implementing the graduation requirements, including the co-development of:

> • **standards alignment & threshold for earning credit**
>
> • criteria for measuring proficiency of the standards
>
> • criteria for performance assessments and potential bank of common assessment resources
>
> • monitoring systems
>
> • publication of comprehensive guidance.

The following tables provide *a model* of how the NGSS can be mapped to the science statewide graduation requirements.

# Challenges and Considerations

- States vary in their adoption and implementation of the NGSS

- Challenging to find specific standards expectations to credits/courses at state level 

- Did not find a way around determining some standards as "high-leverage" standards 

- Most science performance expectations are written with specific identification of SEP

- If integrated physical science credit is not offered, it is challenging for students to have all performance expectations met within three credits

- How to account for standards from multiple domains earned in a given pathway and apply toward credit-earning

1\. **Conceptual Progressions Model** (grades 6–8 and 9–12) – The grade banded PEs are organized so that student understanding of concepts is built progressively throughout the course sequence. This model maps PEs into courses based on what concepts are needed for support without focusing on keeping disciplines separate.

2\. **Science Domains Model** (grades 6–8 and 9–12) – The grade banded PEs are organized into content-specific courses that match the three science domains of the Framework: Physical Science, Life Science, and Earth and Space Science. Since the Engineering domain is mostly integrated into the other three disciplines in the NGSS, it was integrated in these course models rather than presented as a separate course in this sequence. (The four stand-alone engineering PEs in each of the grade bands are connected to all three courses at both levels.)

3\. **Modified Science Domains Model** (grades 9–12) – The 9–12 grade band performance expectations are organized into content-specific courses that match a common high school course sequence of biology, chemistry, and physics. To ensure all students have access to all standards, the PEs connected to the Earth and Space Science domain of the Framework are divided among these courses. It was included as a model for comparison because it is currently a common sequence in high schools across the United States.
