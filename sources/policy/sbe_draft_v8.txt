I. Purpose
The purpose of Vermont’s graduation requirements is to establish common statewide requirements
for earning a high school diploma, while preserving multiple pathways through which students may
acquire and demonstrate the required learning. The statewide graduation requirements operate in
conjunction with the applicable standards adopted by the Vermont State Board of Education (SBE)
pursuant to 16 V.S.A. § 164(9), which establish what students are expected to know and be able to
do.

The statewide graduation requirements are intended to ensure that a Vermont high school diploma:

    •   Promotes substantially equal educational opportunities for all Vermont public school
        students.
    •   Represents a consistent and rigorous level of learning regardless of the school or
        supervisory union/district.
    •   Aligns graduation expectations with Vermont’s Education Quality Standards (EQS) and
        Portrait of a Graduate, and prepares graduates for postsecondary education, careers, civic
        participation, and continued learning.
    •   Reﬂects Vermont’s commitment to proﬁciency-based education, personalized learning,
        ﬂexible pathways, and transferable skills.

II. Guiding Principles
The following principles provide the framework for interpreting and implementing the statewide
graduation requirements. They clarify how statewide expectations should operate in practice,
including the relationship among common requirements, proﬁciency in standards adopted by the
SBE pursuant to 16 V.S.A. § 164(9), local ﬂexibility, assessment, and multiple pathways to
demonstrating learning.

Principle 1: Common expectations, multiple pathways
All Vermont public school students are subject to the same statewide graduation requirements
established in this document. These requirements specify the areas and amount of learning,
expressed in credits, that students must successfully demonstrate to earn a Vermont high school
diploma. The knowledge, skills, and level of proﬁciency required to earn credit are established
through priority standards identiﬁed by the Vermont Agency of Education (AOE) and aligned with
the applicable standards adopted by the SBE pursuant to 16 V.S.A. § 164(9), and through

associated performance indicators used to determine whether students have demonstrated
proﬁciency.

While the statewide graduation requirements are common, the pathways through which students
acquire and demonstrate the learning required to earn those credits may vary. Students may meet
graduation requirements through a range of flexible pathways, including traditional high school
coursework, Career and Technical Education (CTE), dual enrollment, Early College, virtual learning,
work-based learning, internships, community-based learning, independent study, or other
approved learning experiences.

Principle 2: Learning, not time
Graduation requirements are based on demonstrated learning rather than time spent in
instruction. A credit is earned by demonstrating the academic content and proﬁciency in the SBE
adopted student standards identiﬁed for that credit. A credit may not be awarded solely based on
seat time, course completion, or completion of a prescribed number of instructional hours.

Principle 3: Consistent expectations, ﬂexible learning and assessment
Statewide consistency requires that students demonstrate proﬁciency in the applicable priority
standards identiﬁed for each credit, regardless of where or how the learning occurs. It does not
require uniform courses, instructional methods, schedules, learning experiences, or assessments
across schools and districts.

Districts may determine how students are provided opportunities to acquire and demonstrate the
learning required for graduation, provided that local approaches are consistent with standards
adopted by the SBE and with frameworks established by the AOE to support consistent and
comparable application of the statewide graduation requirements across educational settings and
pathways.

Regardless of the educational setting or pathway, the evidence used to award a credit must
demonstrate proficiency in the applicable priority standards, as determined using the applicable
performance indicators.

Graduation Requirement
Graduation requirement means an area and amount of learning, expressed in credits, that a
student must successfully demonstrate to earn a Vermont high school diploma. Statewide
graduation requirements establish the credits students must earn within speciﬁed areas of
learning. The knowledge and skills students must demonstrate to earn those credits are
established through the applicable learning aligned with the applicable priority standards and
performance indicators.

Performance Indicators
Performance indicators mean descriptions of observable and measurable knowledge, skills, and
student performance that specify what students must demonstrate to establish proﬁciency in the
applicable priority standards. Performance indicators may be speciﬁed within State Board-adopted
standards or identiﬁed by the AOE, as necessary, to support consistent determination of
proﬁciency for purposes of awarding credit toward statewide graduation requirements.

Proﬁciency
Proﬁciency means a student’s demonstrated ability to meet the knowledge, skills, and level of
performance required by the applicable priority standards. A student demonstrates proﬁciency for
purposes of earning credit when the student provides suicient evidence, evaluated using the
applicable performance indicators, to demonstrate proﬁciency in the applicable priority standards.

Evidence may be generated through multiple forms of assessment and through multiple learning
settings, including: (a) locally developed or common assessments; (b) performance tasks, projects,
demonstrations, or portfolios; and (c) industry-recognized assessments or demonstrations.

Standards
Standards means the academic content and performance expectations adopted by the SBE
pursuant to 16 V.S.A. §164(9) that establish what students are expected to know and be able to do
within speciﬁed content areas and transferable skills. State Board-adopted standards guide
curriculum and instruction and provide the foundation from which the learning required for
graduation is identiﬁed.

Priority standards mean the essential knowledge and skills, identiﬁed by the AOE from within the
applicable standards adopted by the SBE, that students must demonstrate for purposes of
satisfying statewide graduation requirements. Priority standards identify the learning required for
graduation within a content area and do not replace or limit the broader body of State Board-
adopted standards that guide curriculum and instruction.

IV. Statewide Graduation Requirements
All Vermont public school students must satisfy the statewide graduation requirements established
in Exhibit 1 (below) to earn a Vermont high school diploma. The requirements are expressed in
credits. Credits may be earned through courses or other approved learning experiences and
pathways, consistent with the requirements for awarding credit established in this document.

The statewide graduation requirements identify the areas and amounts of learning students must
successfully demonstrate for graduation. They do not replace or supersede the academic content
standards adopted by the SBE pursuant to 16 V.S.A. § 164(9). The learning students must
demonstrate to earn credit within each graduation requirement is established through the
applicable priority standards and performance indicators.

V. Awarding Credit Toward Statewide Graduation Requirements
This section establishes the requirements for determining when a student’s demonstrated learning
may be recognized and awarded as credit toward statewide graduation requirements. These
requirements apply regardless of where or how the learning occurs and are intended to ensure that
credits awarded across schools, educational settings, and pathways are aligned with SBE-adopted
standards while preserving ﬂexibility in how students acquire and demonstrate the required
learning.

Requirements
A credit may be awarded toward the statewide graduation requirements when the following
requirements are met.

    1. The knowledge, skills, and proﬁciency required for the credit are established through
       applicable priority standards and performance indicators.

        The priority standards applicable to a credit must be identiﬁed from within and aligned with
        the applicable standards adopted by the SBE pursuant to 16 V.S.A. § 164(9). Applicable



Vermont Statewide Graduation Requirements – Working Draft (v.8, October 20, 2026)                       5
        performance indicators shall specify the knowledge, skills, and performance students must
        demonstrate to establish proﬁciency in those priority standards. This requirement applies
        regardless of the educational setting, learning experience, or pathway through which the
        student acquires or demonstrates the learning required for the credit.

        For credit earned through regular high school coursework, supervisory unions and school
        districts may determine the curriculum, learning experiences, assessments, and evidence
        through which students demonstrate proﬁciency, consistent with the applicable State
        Board-adopted standards and statewide graduation requirements.

        For credit earned through Career and Technical Education (CTE), dual enrollment, Early
        College, work-based learning, virtual learning, internships, community-based learning,
        independent study, interdisciplinary learning, or other approved learning experiences, the
        learning demonstrated must be aligned with the academic content and proﬁciency in the
        SBE adopted student standards required for the applicable credit. The AOE shall develop
        frameworks, crosswalks, or other mechanisms, as necessary, to support consistent
        alignment of learning across educational settings and pathways with the applicable State
        Board-adopted standards.

    2. A student has demonstrated proﬁciency.

        A student earns credit by demonstrating proﬁciency in the applicable priority standards, as
        determined using the applicable performance indicators. Credit may not be awarded solely
        based on seat time, course completion, or completion of a prescribed number of
        instructional hours.

        Evidence used to award credit must be suicient to demonstrate that the student has met
        the applicable academic content and proﬁciency in the SBE adopted student standards.
        The form of the evidence and the assessment used to evaluate it may vary, consistent with
        the standards and any applicable statewide requirements or frameworks.

Additional Considerations
When awarding a credit toward the statewide graduation requirements, schools and districts shall
recognize demonstrated learning in ways that allow students to apply evidence across learning
experiences and pathways, consistent with the following provisions.

    •   Learning may satisfy more than one statewide graduation requirement.

        A course, program, project, CTE experience, work-based learning experience,
        interdisciplinary experience, or other approved learning experience may provide evidence
        toward more than one statewide graduation requirement when the student demonstrates
        the academic content and proﬁciency in the SBE adopted student standards required for
        each credit awarded. Credit shall be awarded based on the learning demonstrated rather
        than the title, organizational structure, instructional setting, or duration of the learning
        experience.


Vermont Statewide Graduation Requirements – Working Draft (v.8, October 20, 2026)                      6
    •   A student may accumulate evidence toward meeting a graduation requirement across
        learning experiences.

        Evidence used to demonstrate the academic content and proﬁciency in the SBE adopted
        student standards required for a credit may be developed through more than one course,
        program, educational setting, or learning experience. A student shall not be required to
        demonstrate the same learning more than once when suicient evidence of proﬁciency has
        already been established.

    •   Partial credit toward a graduation requirement may be awarded for demonstrated
        learning.

        When a student demonstrates an identiﬁed portion of the academic content and
        proﬁciency in the SBE adopted student standards required for a credit, partial credit may be
        awarded consistent with applicable State Board standards and frameworks or guidance
        established by the AOE. The amount of credit awarded must correspond to the scope of the
        academic content and proﬁciency in the SBE adopted student standards demonstrated.

    •   Evidence may contribute toward more than one credit for a graduation requirement.

        The same body of evidence may be used to demonstrate academic content or proﬁciency in
        the SBE adopted student standards associated with more than one credit when the
        evidence is suicient to meet the requirements associated with each credit. Awarding
        credit shall be based on what the evidence demonstrates, rather than on the number or
        type of learning experiences in which the student participated.

The statewide graduation requirements are intended to provide students with multiple pathways
through which to acquire and demonstrate the learning required for graduation. The requirements
for awarding credit established in Section V apply regardless of the educational setting or pathway
through which the learning occurs.

This section addresses the application of statewide graduation requirements to CTE, work-based
learning, dual enrollment, Early College, and other approved educational pathways. Where
necessary, the AOE shall develop and maintain frameworks, crosswalks, guidance, or other
mechanisms to ensure that learning demonstrated through these pathways is aligned with the
applicable standards adopted by the SBE and is recognized consistently toward the statewide
graduation requirements.

B. Work-Based Learning
Students may demonstrate academic content and proﬁciency in the SBE adopted student
standards required for statewide graduation credit through approved work-based learning
experiences when those experiences provide opportunities to acquire and demonstrate learning
associated with the applicable graduation requirement.

Participation in or completion of a work-based learning experience does not, by itself, constitute
demonstration of proﬁciency or result in the awarding of credit. Credit may be awarded when
evidence generated through the experience demonstrates the academic content and proﬁciency in
the SBE adopted student standards required for the applicable credit, consistent with Section V.

The AOE shall develop and maintain frameworks, crosswalks, or guidance, as necessary, to support
consistent recognition of learning demonstrated through work-based learning. Such frameworks
will address:

    •   Alignment of work-based learning experiences with applicable State Board-adopted
        standards;

    •   Identiﬁcation of the academic content and proﬁciency in the SBE adopted student
        standards that may be demonstrated through the experience;
    •   Development of learning objectives aligned with the applicable standards;
    •   Acceptable forms of evidence of proﬁciency;
    •   Criteria for assessing and verifying proﬁciency;
    •   Roles and responsibilities of educators, work-based learning coordinators, employers, and
        other partners; and
    •   Documentation and recognition of credit.

C. Dual Enrollment and Early College
Students may apply learning demonstrated through approved dual enrollment and Early College
coursework toward statewide graduation requirements when the coursework provides evidence
that the student has demonstrated the academic content and proﬁciency in the SBE adopted
student standards required for the applicable credit.

Successful completion of a postsecondary course does not necessarily determine the speciﬁc
statewide graduation requirement toward which the credit shall be applied. The relationship
between the postsecondary course and the applicable statewide graduation requirement shall be
determined based on the academic content and proﬁciency in the SBE adopted student standards
demonstrated through the course.

The AOE shall develop and maintain frameworks, crosswalks, guidance, or other mechanisms, as
necessary, to support consistent recognition of dual enrollment and Early College coursework
toward statewide graduation requirements across supervisory unions and school districts. Such
frameworks will address:

    •   Alignment of postsecondary coursework with statewide graduation requirements;
    •   Determination of which graduation requirement or requirements a postsecondary course
        may satisfy;
    •   Conversion or application of postsecondary credit toward high school graduation credit;
    •   Documentation and transfer of credit; and
    •   Consistent recognition of coursework across supervisory unions and school districts.

D. Other Approved Educational Pathways and Learning Experiences
Students may acquire and demonstrate the learning required for statewide graduation credit
through other approved educational pathways and learning experiences, including virtual learning,
internships, community-based learning, independent study, interdisciplinary learning, service
learning, apprenticeships, and other ﬂexible learning opportunities.

Credit toward a statewide graduation requirement may be awarded when the student demonstrates
the academic content and proﬁciency in the SBE adopted student standards required for the
applicable credit, consistent with Section V.

Where necessary to ensure consistent recognition of learning across schools and districts, the AOE
shall develop and maintain frameworks, crosswalks, guidance, or other mechanisms for

ﬂexibility in the design of learning experiences and the forms of evidence through which students
demonstrate proﬁciency while providing suicient consistency to ensure that credit earned through
dierent pathways is recognized comparably toward statewide graduation requirements.

VII. Recognition and Transfer of Credit
Credit awarded toward a statewide graduation requirement in accordance with these standards
shall be recognized toward the same statewide graduation requirement by Vermont public schools
and supervisory unions or districts.

The AOE shall establish and maintain requirements or guidance for the documentation, transfer,
and recognition of credits, including partial credits and credits earned through CTE, work-based
learning, dual enrollment, Early College, and other approved educational pathways. Such
requirements or guidance shall support consistent transcript and reporting practices and ensure
that students are not required to repeat learning for which they have already demonstrated
proﬁciency and received credit in accordance with these standards.

IX.Accessibility and Application to Students with Disabilities
The statewide graduation requirements shall be implemented in accordance with applicable
federal and state law governing students with disabilities and with accessibility requirements
established in Vermont's Education Quality Standards.

Students with disabilities shall have access to the accommodations, supports, services, and
accessible instructional and assessment practices necessary to provide equitable opportunities to
acquire and demonstrate the learning required for graduation.

Accommodations may aect how a student accesses instruction or demonstrates proﬁciency but
do not, by themselves, modify the academic content and proﬁciency in the SBE adopted student
standards required to earn credit toward the statewide graduation requirements.

The AOE shall develop and maintain guidance regarding the application of statewide graduation
requirements to students with disabilities, including guidance concerning accommodations,
accessible forms of assessment and evidence, individualized education programs, and the
requirements applicable to the awarding of a regular high school diploma.                             Commented [TK6]: For committee discussion:

B. Vermont Agency of Education
The AOE shall develop and maintain the frameworks, crosswalks, guidance, and other mechanisms
necessary to support implementation of the statewide graduation requirements and the consistent
recognition of credit across educational settings and pathways.

Consistent with the statewide graduation requirements, the AOE shall, as necessary:

    •   Identify and maintain the priority standards within applicable State Board-adopted
        standards that establish the knowledge and skills students must demonstrate for purposes
        of satisfying statewide graduation requirements;
    •   Identify and maintain performance indicators, where they are not already speciﬁed within
        applicable State Board-adopted standards, for determining whether students have
        demonstrated proﬁciency in the applicable priority standards;
    •   Develop frameworks and crosswalks to support alignment of learning demonstrated
        through CTE, work-based learning, dual enrollment, Early College, and other approved
        educational pathways with applicable State Board-adopted standards and statewide
        graduation requirements;
    •   Establish guidance and criteria to support consistent and comparable recognition of
        learning and evidence of proﬁciency across educational settings and pathways;
    •   Establish guidance for the documentation, transfer, and recognition of credits, including
        partial credits and credits earned through multiple educational settings or learning
        experiences;
    •   Provide guidance regarding the application of transferable skills within the statewide
        graduation requirements;
    •   Provide technical assistance and professional learning to support implementation; and
    •   Monitor implementation of the statewide graduation requirements and identify areas in
        which additional guidance, frameworks, or support are needed.

C. Supervisory Unions and School Districts
Supervisory unions and school districts retain responsibility for curriculum, instruction, learning
experiences, and assessment within regular high school coursework, consistent with applicable
State Board-adopted standards and statewide graduation requirements.

Supervisory unions and school districts shall:

    •   Provide students with opportunities to acquire and demonstrate the academic content and
        proﬁciency in the SBE adopted student standards necessary to satisfy the statewide
        graduation requirements;

    •   Award credit based on demonstrated proﬁciency and in accordance with the requirements
        established in Section V;
    •   Provide students access to multiple educational pathways through which they may satisfy
        statewide graduation requirements, consistent with applicable law and rule;
    •   Recognize and apply credit earned through CTE, work-based learning, dual enrollment,

        standards and applicable AOE frameworks;

    •   Provide students and families with clear information about statewide and local graduation
        requirements and the pathways available to satisfy them.

Supervisory unions and school districts may establish graduation requirements in addition to the
statewide graduation requirements, provided that additional local requirements are consistent with
applicable law and rule and do not modify or reduce the statewide requirements.
