# Vermont High School Course Catalogs and the Proposed Graduation Requirements

Nov 16, 2026 · @Josh Blumberg

## Executive summary

**Most of Vermont's public high schools already offer something in every area the proposals name. Whether those courses *count* is still undefined.** Of the 50 schools whose catalogs can be assessed, **6** offer every Complete-plan credit through courses that clearly qualify. **21** do so if AOE accepts courses that are "a unit or two away" (Environmental Science, Integrated Math, split Algebra I). **23** do so if AOE accepts any related course. The Transitional plan raises those numbers to 14, 37 and 40.

This report doesn't audit schools or pick winners. It lists the definitions AOE and the SBE still have to write and shows which named schools each choice would affect.

### Decisions that move the most schools

1. **Health 1.0 credit.** 22 schools offer only a 0.5-credit Health course (Craftsbury offers 0.25). 33 of the 42 local requirements that name Health ask for less than 1.0. Under the Transitional plan's combined 1.5 PE/Health, only Blue Mountain, Craftsbury and Woodstock still fall short.
2. **Does Environmental Science count as Earth/Space?** 13 schools reach Earth/Space only through Environmental Science, AP Environmental Science, integrated science, ecology, forestry or agriculture science. The answer depends on which NGSS Earth and Space Science performance expectations a course must address, and that is still to be defined. Spaulding has no Earth/Space course at all; its Grade 9 Physical Science teaches "chemistry, earth science, and climate sciences" together. Champlain Valley, Missisquoi Valley and Woodstock offer only a semester.
3. **Do Integrated Math 1 and 2 count as Algebra I and Geometry?** 13 schools have no stand-alone, one-year Algebra I. Burlington, Harwood, Mt. Abraham, Richford, Rutland, South Burlington, Vergennes, Arlington, BFA Fairfax, Champlain Valley and Randolph teach integrated or "Math 1/2" sequences. Hartford and Leland & Gray split Algebra I into Parts A/B or 1/2. Without an equivalency rule, these schools can't tell students or families what counts.
4. **Elective room, and CTE students in particular.** The Complete plan leaves **3.0** open elective credits. The median local requirement today is **7.0**. A two-year CTE program usually carries **6–8** high-school credits, so CTE students can finish only if their embedded credits map to the *new* categories, for example Physical vs. Life vs. Earth/Space science rather than just "science."
5. **Civics as its own course.** Montpelier, Hartford, Middlebury, Otter Valley and Springfield reach Civics only through related courses (social movements, law, IB Global Politics, combined courses). South Burlington and Stowe offer only a semester.
6. **What a "credit" is.** Five schools are proficiency-only (BFA Fairfax, Harwood, Mt. Mansfield, Twinfield, U-32). Block schedules make a semester course worth a full credit at Brattleboro and the Quarry Valley schools. A credit count of 22.5 means different things in different buildings.

### Complete vs. Transitional at a glance

| Threshold for a course to count | Complete (Class of 2033+): schools meeting every requirement | Transitional (Classes of 2031–32) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Strict**: only courses that clearly qualify today | 6 of 50 | 14 of 50 |
| **Moderate**: also courses a unit or two away (Environmental Science, Integrated Math, split Algebra I, interdisciplinary courses) | 21 of 50 | 37 of 50 |
| **Loose**: also embedded, below-level or support courses | 23 of 50 | 40 of 50 |

Most of the gap between the two plans comes from three things. The Transitional plan combines PE and Health into 1.5 credits, moves Statistics/Algebra II to electives, and drops the World Language requirement.

### What catalogs can and can't tell us

Catalogs show what a school **offers**. They don't show whether a course **runs**, how many sections run, or how many students take it. A course listed every other year, or one that runs only when enough students enroll, looks the same as a core course with eight sections. Every count in this report is a count of offerings. Joining this work to **Ed-Fi section and enrollment data** is the next step. Until then, read "offers" as "lists in its catalog."

## Purpose, audience and sources

**This report exists to surface the hard calls AOE and the SBE must make in each subject area, using evidence from every public high school's own catalog.** It is written for Dr. Erin Davis's team at AOE, the State Board and AOE's math, science and social studies content specialists.

**Proposals compared** (credit amounts in the table below):

- **Complete Recommendation**, for the Class of 2033 and later: 22.5 credits.
- **Transitional Flexibility**, for the Classes of 2031–32: 22.5 credits.

**Coverage:** 56 public high schools (CTE centers, independent schools and town academies are out of scope).

- 54 schools have a catalog on file, with 5,566 school courses plus 495 CTE-center listings.
- **50 are assessable.** Oxbow's catalog is a Canva design with no course list. Mill River and Rivendell list titles only. Cabot's is a 16-course partial. These four appear in Appendix A as "data incomplete," never as gaps.
- Enosburg Falls and Williamstown publish no catalog.

**How a course was judged:** each course description was compared, by content, with the requirement definitions in the AOE documents.

- **strong**: the description clearly covers the requirement.
- **possible**: the course is close, but whether it counts depends on a definition AOE hasn't written yet.
- What a catalog *claims* a course fulfills is recorded separately.
- Classification was calibrated against a human answer key: 85% exact agreement on 97 pilot courses, and 93% on 30 held-out courses.

## Theme 1 — Subjects missing entirely from a school

**Only two assessable schools lack a whole required subject: Canaan has no world language, and Spaulding has no Earth/Space course.** That is good news. The bigger risk is the many schools where one course, and probably one teacher, carries an entire requirement.

### What the catalogs show

| School (2024-25 enrollment) | Missing area | Nearest course the catalog offers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Canaan Schools (184, grades 7–12) | World Language / ASL | *Art of World Cultures*, an art course, not language study |
| Spaulding Union HS (610) | Earth/Space Science | *Physical Science* (grade 9), which teaches "the basics of chemistry, earth science, and climate sciences" as one course |
| Cabot School (150) | Financial Literacy, Civics, Earth/Space | Data incomplete: 16-course partial catalog |
| Rivendell Academy (209) | Financial Literacy, Civics, Earth/Space | Data incomplete: titles only |

Spaulding's case shows the definitional problem in a single course. The Grade 9 *Physical Science* description says students will study "thermodynamics in the earth system... molecular processes in the earth's system," and "how to solve problems from sea level rise from global melting in an arctic community." It already carries Earth content. Whether it counts depends on whether AOE allows an integrated course to split its credit between Physical and Earth/Space (Theme 5).

### Single-course dependence

Many schools meet a requirement through exactly one course. If that course stops running, the school has a gap.

| Requirement | Schools with exactly one qualifying course | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Civics | 26 | Proctor (116 students): *Civics*; Winooski (182): *Civics*; Craftsbury: *AP U.S. Government* is the only Civics course |
| Earth/Space | 19 | Hazen: *Astronomy and Rocket Science*; Peoples Academy: *Astronomy*; Randolph: *Integrated Science III: Space Physics*; Otter Valley: *Geophysics* |
| Health | 15 | Usually a single required Health or Wellness course |
| World Studies | 9 | Poultney, Proctor and West Rutland: a shared *Global Studies* course |

Two patterns stand out:

- **Some schools rely on a narrow Earth/Space course.** At several schools the only Earth/Space course is Astronomy, which addresses NGSS Earth's Place in the Universe (ESS1) but not Earth's Systems (ESS2) or Earth and Human Activity (ESS3). If AOE requires the whole Earth/Space domain, those schools have a gap.
- **Small schools depend on AP courses.** At Craftsbury, Lake Region and Mt. Mansfield, the only course that clearly qualifies for Civics is AP U.S. Government. Requiring Civics for every student would put every student in an AP course, or force a new course.

### The decision

AOE must decide whether access counts: may a school meet a requirement through VTVLC, dual enrollment or a neighboring school? This matters most for Canaan (World Language) and for small schools that run one section of a required course.

**Caveat:** catalogs don't say how often a single course runs. Ed-Fi section data would show which of these "one course" requirements are one section a year.

## Theme 2 — Today's local graduation requirements vs. the proposals

**Most schools would need to require more Health, more math, more arts and a world language, and fewer electives.** The proposed 22.5 total is lower than most schools require today. 34 of the 42 schools that state a total require more than 22.5 (median 24). Only Champlain Valley, Craftsbury, Hazen, Twin Valley (22.0) and Northfield (21.5) require less.

&#91;embedded content: phase4/local\_vs\_proposed.csv · schools that state a local requirement for the area (39–47 per area); World Language counts schools with no requirement as below\]

### Where local requirements fall short of the Complete plan

- **Math 4.0:** only 9 schools require four math credits today: Bellows Falls, Blue Mountain, Canaan, Lake Region, Leland & Gray, Peoples Academy and the three Quarry Valley schools (Poultney, Proctor, West Rutland). Most require 3.0, which matches the Transitional plan.
- **Health 1.0:** 33 of 42 schools require 0.5. The 9 that already require 1.0 are Bellows Falls, Blue Mountain, Middlebury, Peoples Academy, Poultney, Proctor, Springfield, West Rutland and Winooski.
- **Arts 1.5:** 37 of 45 schools require 1.0.
- **World Language 1.0:** only 14 schools require one: Bellows Falls, Burlington, Craftsbury, Essex, Green Mountain, Middlebury, Mill River, Milton, Poultney, Proctor, Springfield, West Rutland, Windsor and Winooski.
- **Electives:** every school that lists an elective requirement asks for more than 3.0 (median 7.0, range 3.5–10). 25 schools ask for more than the Transitional plan's 6.0.

### Science and social studies: same total, new internal split

Science and social studies totals already match. 39 of 47 schools require 3.0 science and 29 of 46 require 3.0 social studies. The change is inside the total:

- **Science:** today a school's 3.0 is usually "Biology + two more." The proposals require one each of Physical, Life and Earth/Space. Some catalogs label courses differently from the proposals: Essex and Hartford both list *Earth & Space Science* as meeting their **physical** science requirement.
- **Social studies:** many schools require "Global Citizenship" or "Social Studies" with only U.S. History named. Danville adds Civics for the Class of 2029; Mt. Anthony requires "Civics / Financial Literacy 1 credit" from the Class of 2029.

### Local requirements the proposals would push out

20 schools have a local requirement that has no home in the proposals. With 3.0 open electives, each would compete with every other elective:

- **Technology / Computer Science:** Richford requires *Computer Science 1.0* and *Consumer Economics 0.5*. Rutland requires *Information Technology 1.0* and *Life Skills 0.5*. Milton, Mt. Anthony and Vergennes require Technology 0.5. Canaan and Essex require Computer Applications 0.5. South Burlington requires Information Technology 0.5.
- **Capstones and service:** Windsor requires a Jr. Seminar/Capstone (2.0). Champlain Valley requires RISE (2.0) and a Senior Exit Experience. Blue Mountain requires a Capstone (1.0). Middlebury requires Theory of Knowledge. Lake Region and Milton require community service or internships.
- **Business:** Missisquoi Valley requires Business Education 1.0.

### What a credit means

These comparisons assume that "1 credit" means the same thing everywhere. It doesn't:

- **Brattleboro** counts 96 BUHS units, where a semester block course is 4 units (about 24 standard credits).
- **Poultney, Proctor and West Rutland** (Quarry Valley) and **Peoples Academy** count a semester course as 1 credit. Their totals of 28 and 34 reflect block schedules, not twice the coursework.
- **Danville** counts semesters (42).
- **Harwood, Randolph, South Burlington, Winooski and Woodstock** state no credit total. The five proficiency-only schools graduate students on demonstrated proficiencies, and a credit count is secondary there.

**The decision:** the proposals need a definition of a credit (seat time, proficiency, or both) before 22.5 can be compared across schools.

## Theme 3 — The "possible" courses: where AOE sets the bar

**Where AOE sets the bar is the single choice that moves the most schools.** Under the Complete plan it is the difference between 6 and 23 schools that can offer every requirement. Set it too low and standards don't rise. Set it too high and courses that are creative, interdisciplinary or built for students who aren't yet ready for the standard course stop counting. Some students then can't graduate on time.

We judged 1,251 course-to-requirement links as **possible**: close to a requirement, but not clearly inside it. This report tests three thresholds:

| Tier | What counts | Complete: schools meeting every requirement | Transitional |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Strict** | Only courses whose description clearly covers the requirement | 6 | 14 |
| **Moderate** | Also courses "a unit or two away": Environmental Science for Earth/Space, Integrated Math, split Algebra I, interdisciplinary courses, applied/technical courses, unclear grade level | 21 | 37 |
| **Loose** | Also embedded content, below-level and support courses | 23 | 40 |

The tiers are judged from course descriptions only, and descriptions are easy to change. A teacher can add or drop a unit or two in a full-year course. So under Moderate, many "possible" courses would qualify after a small revision, and **the Moderate tier is about what a school could do with modest effort.**

Strict is different. It would press on three kinds of courses at once:

- courses that are **creative or interdisciplinary** (Windsor's *Earth & the Environment*, Montpelier's *Social Change Theory and Practice*);
- courses **built around a career or place** (Canaan's *EC Forestry Resources*, Danville's *Woodworking*);
- courses that are **not rigorous enough** (Lake Region's *Consumer Mathematics*).

Only the third kind is the target of higher standards.

Health and PE barely move under any threshold. Their shortfall is credit **amount** (0.5 offered vs. 1.0 required), not definition.

### Course examples

**Earth/Space, Moderate tier: Windsor, *Earth & the Environment* (grade 10, 1.0 credit; the catalog says it meets the science requirement).**

> "a full year, interdisciplinary science course … dig deeper into topics such as: Sustainability, Basic Earth Science, Waste Management and Recycling, Green Building, Energy, Air-Water-Soil Quality, Climate Change, Agriculture, and Population Growth."

Earth science is one topic in a list of nine. Adding a unit or two of plate tectonics and the rock cycle could make it qualify. Whether that is enough depends on which NGSS ESS performance expectations AOE attaches to the credit.

**Earth/Space, Moderate tier: Craftsbury, *AP Environmental Science* (Craftsbury's only Earth/Space-type course).**

> "Students investigate ecological processes, energy and resource use, population dynamics, biodiversity, pollution, climate change, and the impacts of human activities on Earth's systems. Through laboratory investigations …"

APES covers NGSS ESS3 (Earth and Human Activity) thoroughly. It touches ESS2 (Earth's Systems), and it covers little of ESS1 (space).

**Civics, Moderate tier: Montpelier, *Social Change Theory and Practice* (1.0 credit). This is Montpelier's closest course to Civics.**

> "In the spring, the students will identify a current policy issue at the district, city, or state level and work together on a class-led campaign."

This is applied civic action, which is the point of civics education. It doesn't describe the structure of government. Under Strict, Montpelier would need a new course.

**Financial Literacy, Loose tier only: Fair Haven, *Adulting 101* (0.5 credit, grades 11–12).**

> "determine the impact of credit cards and loans on their credit score, budget, save and invest their money, and file their taxes. Students will also learn how to manage their time, balance their physical and mental health …"

The memo asks for "stand-alone content." Fair Haven, Mt. Mansfield and Randolph meet Financial Literacy only through embedded content like this.

### Math below Algebra I

**37 schools list 62 math courses that sit below Algebra I** (Pre-Algebra, Essentials, Foundations, Consumer, Applied or Workplace Math).

- **Lake Region** lists four, a three-year track below Algebra I. *Essentials of Mathematics I* is "a review of the operations on whole numbers, fractions, decimals, and percentages." *Essentials of Mathematics II* covers "banking, budget planning, insurance, taxes." *Consumer Mathematics* is "designed as a third year offering for those completing Essentials of Mathematics II." Lake Region also offers *Pre-Algebra*.
- **Blue Mountain, Burlington, Fair Haven and Rutland** each list three. Danville lists *Pre-Algebra* and *Math in the Workplace*.

For this report, Pre-Algebra counts as a math elective (Math Pathway, strong). Consumer and Essentials math are "possible" and may be below high-school level; a math specialist should rule on them.

**The implementation risk:** the Complete plan requires Algebra I, Geometry, Statistics/Algebra II and a Pathway course. A student who starts in Lake Region's Essentials track has room for only one below-level course, and must reach Algebra I by grade 10 to finish four required math credits. **Catalogs don't show how many students are in these tracks. Ed-Fi enrollment is needed to size the problem.**

### When catalog claims and course content disagree

At 77 courses (111 claims), what the catalog says a course fulfills isn't supported by its description. Two causes:

1. **Generic "science credit" today.** Windsor's CTE listings say "Embedded credit: Science." Essex lists *Planetary Astronomy* and *Earth and Space Science* as "Physical Science credit." Under the proposals, each needs a domain: Physical, Life or Earth/Space.
2. **Credit by equivalency.**
   - Danville's *Woodworking* lists "Credit areas: English, Science, Elective," but its description is about "teamwork, leadership, professionalism, craftsmanship."
   - Lake Region's *Programming for the Web in HTML* "may fulfill 1/2 credit of the fine arts graduation requirement."
   - Springfield's *Carpentry II* lists "Embedded credits: 1 Math, 1 Art, 1 Elective."

**The decision:** will AOE require that equivalency credit be backed by documented standards in the course? If so, the 19 schools that make such claims (Fair Haven, Hazen, Richford, Springfield and Windsor most often) will need to rewrite course documents.

### Labs

All three science requirements say "with lab," but no AOE document defines a lab. 418 of 664 science courses (63%) mention labs or hands-on investigation; the other 37% don't say either way. A definition (how many hours, what kind of investigation, whether field work or simulation counts) would decide whether those 246 courses count.

## Theme 4 — Electives: less room than schools offer today

**The Complete plan leaves 3.0 open elective credits. Schools require a median of 7.0 electives today, and 25 schools require more than the Transitional plan's 6.0.** Students would have less room for psychology, engineering, business, a second language, a third year of art, and especially a two-year CTE program.

### What competes for 3.0 credits

**Courses that count toward no requirement.** 419 of the 5,318 regular courses (8%) map to no proposed requirement. They are concentrated in a few areas:

| Kind of course | Courses | Examples (school) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Social-science electives | 135 | *AP Psychology*, *Sociology* (Peoples Academy); *Introduction to Gender Studies*, *Great Ideas in Philosophy* (Essex); *Archaeology*, *Economic Justice PBL* (Randolph) |
| Technology / engineering | 89 | *Design & Engineering Technology 1–3 (CAD/CAM)* (Champlain Valley); *Forged with Fire*, *Ultimate Maker Space* (Peoples Academy); *Small Engines*, *Welding Basics* (Otter Valley) |
| Business | 46 | *Microsoft Excel* (Lake Region); *Entrepreneurship* (Essex); *Business Law* (Champlain Valley) |
| Family & consumer science | 28 | *International Foods* (Burlington, Essex) |
| Computer science | 20 | *Web Design* (Burlington); *Programming for the Web in CSS* (Lake Region) |
| Other (media, design, capstones, driver ed) | 101 | *AI and Society*, *Intro to Design Thinking* (Champlain Valley) |

**Courses beyond the required amount in an area.** Every course past the required credit in an area also comes out of electives: a fifth English course, a second world language, the third arts course, AP Physics after Chemistry. These are the courses that make each school distinctive. Examples include Lake Region's menu of half-credit English electives and Montpelier's place-based courses (*La Bonne Crêpe*, a student-run food cart; *Manufacturing & Student Entrepreneurship*).

### The CTE squeeze: the clearest implementation problem

Where catalogs print a credit value, CTE programs carry 3–4 high-school credits per year (83 of 100 such listings). Most of that is elective credit today:

- **Richford, Construction Technology:** "Year 1: 4 elective credit; Year 2: 1 math credit, 1 practical art credit, 2 elective credit."
- **Mt. Mansfield, Automotive Science and Technology I:** "Year 1 – 2.0 Elective, 0.5 Science, 0.5 English."
- **Hartford, Information Technology:** "1 Technology credit, 0.5 English credit, 1.5 elective credits."

The arithmetic:

- A two-year program carries 6–8 credits; under Complete, 19.5 credits are already required.
- If the program awards mostly elective credit, a CTE student needs **25.5–27.5 credits** to graduate. That is 3–5 more than the 22.5 total.
- The student can finish only if:
  1. the school's schedule has room for 25.5+ credits (catalogs rarely say; see Systems context); or
  2. the CTE program's **embedded credits** count toward named requirements.

Embedded credit is the realistic path, but it must now name the proposals' categories. "Embedded science" (Windsor, Fair Haven) has to become Physical, Life *or* Earth/Space. "Embedded math" has to say whether it is Algebra I, Geometry or Pathway. 17 schools' catalogs already describe embedded or core credit for CTE programs (Bellows Falls, Blue Mountain, Canaan, Fair Haven, Hartford, Hazen, Lamoille, Middlebury, Milton, Missisquoi Valley, Montpelier, Mt. Mansfield, Richford, South Burlington, Springfield, Windsor, Woodstock). Many others list CTE programs by name only, or not at all (Leland & Gray, Spaulding, Vergennes, Winooski).

### The decision

1. Will AOE publish a crosswalk from CTE program standards to the new credit categories? Or will each school and CTE center negotiate its own?
2. Will required courses taken at a CTE center count only if the center's course document names the AOE standards?
3. Should the open-elective floor be higher for CTE completers?

**Caveat:** catalogs list electives, not enrollment. Ed-Fi would show which electives actually run and how many students would lose access.

## Theme 5 — Interdisciplinary courses and fractional credit

**24 schools already run 42 interdisciplinary courses, and they award the credit in four different ways. The proposals don't say how one course may count toward more than one requirement.** Without a rule, the most innovative courses in the state become the hardest to place on a transcript.

### Four ways schools award interdisciplinary credit today

| Model | Schools and courses | How the credit works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Double block, double credit** | Champlain Valley *Core Humanities* (gr. 9) and *Grade 10 Humanities – American Studies*; Colchester *The American Experience*; Essex *Grade 9 Honors Humanities*; Otter Valley *Humanities 9*; Rutland *American Voices*; South Burlington and Bellows Falls *American Studies* | 2.0 credits: 1 English + 1 Social Studies |
| **Split credit in one slot** | BFA St. Albans *PBL Playwriting & Performance*; South Burlington *Holocaust Studies*; Proctor *Comics & Culture* | 0.5 + 0.5, e.g. "half a credit in English and half a credit in Art in one semester" |
| **Either/or** | Windsor *The Making of the Modern World*; Vergennes *Humanities*; Missisquoi Valley *Humanities in Action*; Middlebury *DP Environmental Systems and Societies* | Student chooses which area gets the credit |
| **Bundled program** | Champlain Valley *Project Academy* (4.0: "one in each of English, Math, Social Studies, and Science"); Fair Haven *Outdoor Pursuits* (3.0: Math, Science, PE); Harwood *Community Learning Center* | Several credits from one block of time |

Some catalogs don't say how credit is split: Twinfield's *ELA and SCI Integrated Studies*, Danville's *STEM and LIT*, Mt. Mansfield's *Vermont Sustainability Project* and Lamoille's *History & Science of Mummification*.

**Example: Twinfield, *10th Grade Human Impact on the Biosphere / ELA Integrated* (full year, credit not stated).**

> "Students will then investigate cells, disease, and the ways human activity has influenced the development, spread, and treatment of infectious and noninfectious diseases. The course concludes with an in-depth study of climate change …"

This one course touches ELA Introductory, Life Science (cells, disease) and Earth/Space (climate change). Under a whole-credit rule it can be only one of them, so Twinfield loses its route to Life Science (Twinfield shows Life Science as possible-only).

### Fractional credit is normal, but not evenly spread

Share of courses printed as 0.5 credit:

| Area | Half-credit share |
| --- | --- |
| PE & Health | 76% |
| Arts | 58% |
| ELA | 37% |
| Social Studies | 35% |
| Science | 20% |
| Math | 10% |
| World Language | 3% |

Half-credit courses are how small schools offer variety. Lake Region's English electives and Missisquoi Valley's semester *CP Earth and Space Science* are examples. A requirement stated as "1.0 Earth/Space" can be met with two semesters. But at Champlain Valley, Missisquoi Valley and Woodstock, only a single semester of Earth/Space is offered, so these schools show as *short*.

### Integrated science and CTE as partial credit

- **Integrated science 9:** Green Mountain *Integrated Science 9*, Milton *Physical & Earth Science*, Mt. Abraham *Science 9: Physical Sciences & Earth Systems*, U-32 *9th Grade Earth/Physical Science* and Spaulding *Physical Science* each combine Physical and Earth content. If a course could award 0.5 Physical + 0.5 Earth/Space, these five schools would be halfway to the Earth/Space credit. Each would still need a second half-credit, for example from an environmental or astronomy elective.
- **CTE:** 13 schools' catalogs state embedded core credit for 133 CTE listings. Springfield/River Valley and Windsor give the most detail ("Embedded credits: 1 Math, 1 Science, 1 English").

### The decision

AOE needs a rule for how a course counts toward several areas. Options:

1. **Whole credit only:** a course counts toward one area. Simple to audit, but it penalizes the 24 schools above and most CTE programs.
2. **Declared fractions:** the school states the split in the course document (0.5/0.5) and maps standards to each part. This matches what BFA St. Albans and South Burlington already do.
3. **Proficiency-based:** credit follows the proficiencies demonstrated. This fits the five proficiency-only schools, but it is hardest to compare across schools.

This report doesn't address schools awarding several credits for one course as a possible weakening of rigor. That is flagged as a separate study.

## Theme 6 — Integrated Math as Algebra I and Geometry

**The proposals name Algebra I and Geometry as courses. 11 Vermont high schools don't teach them as separate courses, and two more spread Algebra I over two years.** Until AOE publishes an equivalency rule, these 13 schools can't tell families whether their core math sequence satisfies the requirement.

| Harwood | *Integrated Math 1 / 2* | possible only | possible only |

| Richford | *Integrated Math I / II* | possible only | possible only |

| BFA Fairfax | *Math 1: Linear and Exponential Functions*, *Math 2: Quadratic Functions* | possible only | covered |
| Champlain Valley | *Core Math 1 Foundations*, *Core Math 1*, *Math 2* | possible only | covered |

| Hartford | *Algebra 1 – Part A / Part B* | possible only (split) | covered |
| Leland & Gray | *Algebra I Part 1 / Part 2* | possible only (split) | covered |

Fair Haven, Montpelier, Mt. Mansfield and Poultney also offer integrated courses, but they offer a traditional Algebra I alongside them.

**Example: Burlington, *Integrated Math 1* (1.0 credit, grades 9–10).**

> "Univariate statistics, Understanding linear functions and their applications, Linear regression, Systems of linear equations and inequalities, Coordinate geometry, Rigid transformations, Triangle congruence, and Exponential functions."

About two-thirds of this is Algebra I content. The rest (transformations, congruence, coordinate geometry) is Geometry, and the statistics strand is not in either requirement. Burlington's catalog says only that "one of the three recommended credits for graduation is Integrated Math."

**Example: Hartford, *Algebra 1 – Part A*.**

> "an emphasis on Solving, Graphing, and Writing Linear Equations and Inequalities, as well as introduce Exponential Functions."

Part A is the first half of Algebra I, taught over a full credit. A student who completes Parts A and B has finished Algebra I, but has spent two credits on it.

### Three possible rules, and what each does

| Rule | Algebra I available (of 50 schools) | Geometry available | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Don't count** integrated or split courses | 37 | 44 | 13 schools must add a traditional course or rebuild their sequence |
| **Full credit:** Integrated Math 1 = Algebra I, Integrated Math 2 = Geometry; Part A + B = Algebra I | 50 | 50 | No school changes. Overstates the match, because content is redistributed across the sequence |
| **Split credit:** each of Integrated Math 1 and 2 = 0.5 Algebra I + 0.5 Geometry | 50 | 50 for students who finish both courses | Same result as full credit for students who finish both courses. Students who stop after Math 1, or transfer, hold 0.5 + 0.5 |

The split rule matches how integrated sequences are designed: Common Core's integrated pathway spreads Algebra I, Geometry and Algebra II content across Math I–III. It raises two questions for the math specialist:

1. **Does Math 1 + Math 2 cover all of the Geometry standards?** In the Common Core integrated pathway, some Geometry (circles, some modeling) sits in Math III. Rutland's *Integrated Math 4* is described as "heavy geometry, trig, circles." A strict standards match could require Math III for the Geometry credit.
2. **How should the two-year split courses count?** Hartford, Leland & Gray and South Burlington's *Math 1 by 2 Years* could award 2.0 credits for one Algebra I. The second credit could count as the Math Pathway elective, or as nothing.

### The decision

AOE should publish an equivalency table covering:

- Integrated Math 1/2/3;
- two-year Algebra I;
- Algebra I "Foundations" and support courses (Champlain Valley's *Core Math 1 Foundations*, Colchester and Montpelier's *Strategic Algebra*);
- the Statistics strand in Integrated Math 1. Does it count toward Statistics/Data Science?

And it should state whether the rule is course-based or standards-based.

## Methods and limitations

**Every number here is a count of catalog offerings, judged from course descriptions against requirement definitions that AOE has not finished writing.** Read the counts as "how many schools depend on each decision," not as compliance.

### How the data were built

1. **Catalogs:** found and downloaded for 54 of 56 schools; each has a retrieval date.
   - 44 are 2026-27.
   - 9 are older: Bellows Falls (2024-26); Cabot, Canaan, Green Mountain, Leland & Gray, Mt. Anthony and North Country (2025-26); the undated Quarry Valley district catalog.
   - Windsor's 2026-27 catalog may be a draft.
2. **Courses:** 6,061 course records were extracted with verbatim descriptions; an automatic check compares each one with the source text.
3. **Classification:** each course was mapped to the requirements by content, as *strong* or *possible*, with a written rationale.
   - What a catalog claims a course fulfills is kept separately and never closes a gap.
   - Accuracy against a human answer key: 85% exact on 97 pilot courses (Danville, Lake Region); 93% on 30 held-out Montpelier courses.
4. **Coverage rules:**
   - A requirement is *covered* when strong courses offer at least the required credits (or, where credits aren't printed, at least that many courses).
   - CTE-center programs and flexible pathways never close a gap.
   - Alternate-year courses **are** counted, because a student can take them within four years. Requirements met only by such a course are flagged.
5. **Corrections made for this report:**
   - Essex *Algebra I 100/200* re-coded as strong (the numbers are levels, not parts).
   - Alternate-year courses are now included. This changed Winooski's Earth/Space from a gap to possible-only.
   - Cabot is treated as data-incomplete.
   - Brattleboro's units are divided by 4.
   - Green Mountain's requirements use the Class of 2028 table.

### Limitations

- **Offerings, not sections or enrollment.** A course listed may not run. This needs the **Ed-Fi** section and enrollment join (the next step) for:
  - how many students take below-Algebra-I math;
  - how many sections of Health or Civics would need to be added;
  - which electives actually run.
- **Descriptions are short.** Most are 2–5 sentences. A course classified "possible" may fully meet the standard in practice, and a teacher may add or drop a unit in any year.
- **Requirement definitions are provisional.** Credit amounts follow the 2026-10-20 memo. Civics, US History and World Studies are assumed to be 1.0 each. "Lab" and "stand-alone" financial literacy are undefined.
- **Thin data:**
  - Oxbow: no course list.
  - Mill River, Rivendell: titles only.
  - Cabot: partial.
  - North Country, Twinfield: partial or read from web pages.
  - Woodstock: no graduation-requirements section.
- **Not in scope:** CTE centers' own catalogs, independent schools and town academies; whether multiple credits for one course weakens rigor (a later study).
