Course Title: Analyzing Film as Literature

Grade Levels: 11-12

Course Length: Semester

Prerequisites: None

Course Description: This course introduces students to the art and analysis of cinema through the close examination of movies from various decades and genres. Through observation and discussion, students will learn to “read” films by developing a critical eye for semiotics (the interpretation of signs and symbols), narrative construction, cinematography, and mise-en-scène (the setting of a scene). They will become active participants in the movie-watching experience by developing the skills to recognize, analyze, evaluate, and enjoy movies as a form of art and communication.  This course is writing intensive. Students will examine various filming techniques and study the effective use of genre, character building, dialogue, plot, theme, motif, and symbolism. They will evaluate each movie on a 5-star scale and write blog-style reviews. They should also expect to write at least four academic papers using specific critical lenses and to craft several creative projects.  **Parent/Guardian signature required if under 18.  Film List: It’s Such a Beautiful Day, Casablanca, A Ghost Story, Parasite, Everything Everywhere All at Once, Arrival, The Grand Budapest Hotel, Fantastic Mr. Fox, Moonrise Kingdom, The Great Gatsby,  Interstellar, Dunkirk, Aftersun, Napoleon Dynamite, Midnight in Paris, Arrival, One Flew Over the Cuckoo’s Nest, True Grit, Knives Out, Bonnie and Clyde, The Maltese Falcon, and The Batman.
