SCHOOL MISSION & EXPECTATIONS FOR LEARNING

Burlington High School is an innovative, equitable, and collaborative community of learners inspiring and shaping a dynamic and sustainable future for Burlington. We build on the diverse cultures, experiences, and interests of our students and community to support student-centered learning and foster intellectual growth. We partner with families and the community-at-large to help our students develop the skills to become independent, self-directed, and lifelong learners who contribute responsibly to our world.  Our mission is to challenge all students to achieve at their highest levels.

RESIDENCY REQUIREMENT

Prospective students must provide verifiable proof of Burlington residence, per Burlington School District enrollment policy, during enrollment registration.

SCHOOL CHOICE

School choice information and directions may be found under Resources on the BHS website. Students interested in applying for School Choice must submit a completed School Choice Application between January 1 and March 1 of each year. Up to ten (10) new slots are available each year. A lottery will be held if more applications are received than slots available.  Students will be informed by April 1 of school choice decisions or thereafter as slots remain available.

STUDENT PROGRAMS & SERVICES

STUDENTS WITH DISABILITIES

Federal legislation requires that all children with disabilities, birth through age 21, receive a free and appropriate public education. BHS seeks to proactively identify all children with disabilities and who are not presently receiving Special Education services.  

SCHOOL COUNSELING

Burlington High School Counseling provides academic planning, college and career planning, and social-emotional support to students and families. Please consult the School Counseling Office for questions regarding courses and course placement by reaching out to your assigned School Counselor. Seniors who will apply to colleges, post-secondary programs, or require documentation support for scholarships and recommendations should be in close consultation with their School Counselor throughout the fall.

STUDENT ASSISTANCE AND SOCIAL WORK SERVICES

BHS has a Social Worker, Mental Health Counselor, and a School Services Clinician through the Howard Center who provide mental health assessment, crisis support, and connection to community resources for ongoing student and family needs. Students may access drug, alcohol, and tobacco counseling services through the Student Assistance and Prevention Counselor. These mental health professionals work individually with students, partner with families, and the school community to address community wellness and socio-emotional challenges.  

EDUCATIONAL SUPPORT TEAM

The purpose of the Educational Support Team (EST), as required by Acts 117 and 230, is to ensure that all BHS students are provided with appropriate educational experiences with the goal of providing for their continuing growth and development, and preparation for independence and productivity as community members.

1:1 TECHNOLOGY

Students are issued a Chromebook as a learning tool. Students must abide by the Acceptable Use Policy and have parent/guardian consent to use Chromebooks at school/home.

ADVISORY

All students are assigned an Advisory period in order to support students’ learning and social-emotional health while at Burlington High School.  Advisory is an opportunity for students and staff to build positive connections with each other and direct support for their Personal Learning Plans (PLPs).

HOMEWORK SUPPORT CENTER

Students may receive support for completing homework and other course assignments in the after-school Homework Support Center located in the BHS Library on Mondays - Thursdays from 3:15 p.m.-5:00 p.m. All students are eligible and encouraged to attend on a drop-in basis as desired. Students who are behind in coursework may be referred by teachers to the center in order to catch up on assignments. Tutors are available to help students understand and complete their work. The Open Access Computer Lab is available for student use during this time.

HIPPOCAMPUS

The Hippocampus is a student-led service organization that operates a peer-tutoring center. Students in 11th and 12th grade may serve as Hippocampus tutors, working to support the academic success of their peers. Tutors organize workshops during Office Hours to help 9th graders with specific assignments and skills, as well as drop-in support every block. Tutors will commit to one (1) or more weekly shift(s) to earn community service hours for their time tutoring. Tutors may be eligible to earn one-half (0.5) elective credit for one year’s service on the Hippocampus leadership team. Students interested in receiving tutoring support or serving as a Hippocampus tutor should contact Mr. Ben Heintz at bheintz@bsdvt.org.

LIBRARY

The BHS Library is open from 7:45 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. (3:15 p.m. on Fridays) to support student academic performance through:

  • selected print and online resources to support research needs at a variety of reading levels
  • before and after school hours for homework, tutoring, and research assistance

YEAR-END STUDIES (YES)

In the fourth and final academic quarter of the school year, all students participate in the Year-End Studies (YES) Session.  Students select one full-day or two half-day, ten-day mini-courses in order to deepen learning interests and apply academic skills and knowledge.  YES provides a variety of new, enriching, and interdisciplinary learning experiences including: on-campus seminars, off-campus field experiences, travel programs, internships, and other learning opportunities. A culminating showcase allows students, parents, and community members to learn from each other and celebrate YES accomplishments. Students will sign up during the third academic quarter and informed of their courses in the spring.  Information on the current year’s offerings can be found on the BHS website.

COMMUNITY SERVICE

All students must complete ten (10) hours of community service per year of enrollment at BHS. Community service prompts students to apply their skills and knowledge locally to learn the responsibility citizens have in a democratic society. Hours may be accrued beginning the summer preceding 9th grade through 12th grade. The deadline to submit community service hours to confirm participation in commencement ceremonies is the last day of Quarter 4 of the student’s graduating year. Students are responsible for tracking their hours and submitting documentation to the School Counseling Office.  

PERSONALIZED LEARNING PLAN (PLP)

In addition to academic credit accrual, students are required to develop and maintain a Personalized Learning Plan (PLP) in order to meet graduation requirements. Students will utilize the PLP to set, review, and document goal progress towards academic proficiencies in consultation with an advisor and their parents/guardians. Students will collect high-quality evidence (e.g. projects, essays, performances, YES, and experiential learning) in a digital portfolio. Students will be provided time and structure in Advisories and classroom courses to regularly update and reflect upon this work.

SCHEDULING INFORMATION & POLICIES

REPEATED CLASSES

Students who have successfully completed a course and earned academic credit in the class may not repeat the course for additional credit. If a course is repeated either here at BHS or through an approved education partner, the new grade will appear on the transcript for purposes of GPA calculations and total credits earned. The original grade will remain on the transcript, but will not count towards the GPA.

CREDIT RECOVERY

Credit recovery opportunities may be offered throughout the year to permit qualifying students to make up academic work and gain academic credit.

MIDDLE SCHOOL CLASSES

Classes completed as part of a standard middle school enrollment and as part of the middle school curriculum (i.e. 8th grade Algebra) do not count toward BHS graduation credit requirements.

CREDIT FOR BURLINGTON TECHNICAL CENTER COURSES

Students who complete two (2) years of a Burlington Technical Center program may be granted up to three (3) embedded credits toward BHS graduation requirements in English, fine arts, math, science, or social studies, per the scope of the program.

PASS/FAIL GRADING

A student wishing to take a course for Pass/Fail must determine the arrangement before the halfway point in the course. Arrangements for this grading option should be made through the School Counseling Office.

ATHLETIC ELIGIBILITY

To foster maximum effort in the classroom and the athletic arena, all student-athletes are required to complete a Blue Form each week throughout the course of their sports season. For more information about maintaining eligibility and Blue Form procedures, please refer to the BHS Student-Athlete Academic Guidelines. Failure to complete a Blue Form by the weekly deadline may impact athletics eligibility and result in the student-athlete sitting out of competition for one week. Student-athletes will still be allowed to participate in practice during these times unless otherwise directed by a coach or athletic administrator.

All students must carry a minimum of six (6) courses to be eligible for athletics. Quarterly grades are used to determine eligibility. Fourth quarter grades from the previous school year will be used in deciding first quarter/fall season eligibility. SCHEDULE CHANGES DURING THE ADD/DROP PERIOD

Students may change their schedules during an adjustment period called Add/Drop at the beginning of each semester. Because ample time is offered during these periods to consider enrollment choices, students may only make schedule changes outside of these times in very unusual circumstances. To implement a schedule change during Add/Drop, a student must:

  1. Discuss the proposed change with their current teacher.
  2. See a counselor to request an Add/Drop form.
  3. Discuss the proposed change with a parent/guardian and have them sign the form.
  4. Return the form to the School Counseling Office by the end of the following school day.
  5. Students must remain in their current course(s) until notified the change is completed.

PROCEDURE FOR DROPPING COURSES AFTER ADD/DROP PERIOD

The process of academic planning represents a cumulative effort on the part of students, parents, faculty, and administrators. Therefore, students are strongly encouraged to commit to the courses that are on their schedules at the beginning of the school year and second semester. Proper placement and ongoing support are essential components of student success. When students find themselves struggling to succeed in a particular class, the best course of action is to seek help from the teacher, the school counselor, and the student’s advisor.  BHS has a variety of supports available.  After Add/Drop periods conclude, course changes during the school year only can be made with confirmation from the parent/guardian and after consultation with the teacher of record and School Counselor. In the event a course drop is necessary, the student’s academic record will be affected in the following manner:

  1. Courses dropped before the deadline will not appear on a student’s report card or transcript and will have no impact on GPA.
  2. Courses dropped after the deadline will appear as a ‘W’/withdrawal on a student’s report card and transcript. A ‘W’ does not impact GPA. The deadlines are as follows:
  1. For year-long courses: The last day of the first marking period (quarter 1).
  2. For semester 1 courses: Halfway through the first marking period (quarter 1).
  3. For semester 2 courses: Halfway through the third marking period (quarter 3).

CHANGING LEVELS IN COURSES

Students who find it necessary to change levels in a full-year course, for instance from Honors Biology to Biology I, must do so no later than the close of the first quarter. Students who change course levels within an academic discipline at BHS during the school year (outside of the Add/Drop period) will have grades, credits, and attendance records follow them into the new course, and a ‘W’/withdrawal  will be recorded on the transcript for the course that is dropped.

AWARD OF PARTIAL CREDIT

In the event of extenuating circumstances (e.g. a student finishes graduation requirements at the end of the first semester or requires a withdrawal from a year-long course at the close of semester 1 (one)  partial credit may be awarded for full-year or semester length courses. Credit award determinations require approval from the Director of Counseling and/or Principal.

CREDIT AWARD DETERMINATIONS FOR OUTSIDE STUDY

Academic credit will only be awarded and listed on the transcript for programs of study from accredited institutions and in cases where this credit is being used toward meeting the requirements for graduation. Prior to starting a course outside of BHS, students wishing to receive credit for coursework outside of BHS should meet with their school counselor to ensure that the course will be eligible to receive credit. Documentation of successful completion of the course provided by the student. COMPUTER SCIENCE

The mission behind our computer science courses is to enable students to be designers and creators of the technology they interact with every day. We use a range of programming languages – beginning with either Scratch or HTML & CSS, and then transitioning to Python, C++ and Java. These courses are as much about developing fluency in a given language as they are about computational thinking skills, problem solving, communication and creativity. Intermediate and advanced courses are anticipated to be offered on an alternating yearly schedule, with AP Computer Science A being offered one year, and Computer Science 2: Programming with Python and Computer Science 3: Physical Computing the next.

COURSES IN COMPUTER SCIENCE

52657

Computer Science I: Creative Computing (Fall Semester)

0.5 Credit (Elective)

Grades 9 - 12

Computer science is an essential part of all our lives, and if you know how to think like a computer scientist, write programs and solve problems, you will be able to create anything you put your mind to. Really, it’s like a super power! This course is as much about programming and learning foundational concepts in computer science as it is about creativity, design and problem solving. We’ll use the programming language Scratch, a block-based language perfect for complete beginners as well as those with some programming experience looking for a place to experiment with creative computing. By the end of the semester students will approach the text-based language Python. Assignments are mostly project based, with freedom to design and build your own original programs. Students earning an 80% or higher in this course may continue to Computer Science II: Programming with Python.

Primary Graduate Expectations: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving; Curiosity & Creativity

52658

Computer Science II: Programming with Python (Spring Semester)

0.5 Credit (Elective)

Grades 9 - 12

In this semester course, you will build on the foundational concepts you learned in Computer Science 1 to build complex programs in a professional, text-based language. Python is a general-purpose programming language that can be used to develop applications, analyze data, automate tasks, and much more. Through an emphasis on computational thinking skills and problem solving, you will learn Python commands, functions and control structures that will serve as the building blocks of your own original programs.

Prerequisite: Successful completion of Computer Science I with at least an 80%, or demonstrate prior programming experience to the teacher to determine course placement.

Primary Graduate Expectations: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving; Curiosity & Creativity

52659

Computer Science III: Physical Computing (Fall Semester)

0.5 Credit (Elective)

Grades 10 - 12

Physical computing forms the basis of smart devices, wearables like smart watches, e-textiles and IoT (Internet of Things) devices. In this semester course, you will learn to design electronic devices that interact with the physical world by building circuits and writing programs that run on microcontrollers (tiny, hand-held computers). You might build a robot arm that can draw, a racetrack that times the speed of a toy car, a small greenhouse that opens a vent if the temperature gets too hot – what you build is up to you! This course is project based and highly self-directed; you will have lots of freedom to pursue your own projects. We will utilize the Arduino microcontroller in projects and the programming language C++.

Prerequisite: Successful completion of Computer Science II with at least an 80% or AP Computer Science A with at least a 70%; or demonstrate prior programming experience to the teacher to determine course placement

Primary Graduate Expectations: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving; Curiosity & Creativity

DESIGN TECHNOLOGY

The mission of Design Technology Education is to educate our students to become technologically literate through the study of human design in an ever-changing technological society.  Design Technology education provides application and relevance to concepts of science, technology, engineering, art, and math (STEAM). Students will develop safe and appropriate skills in a wide range of traditional and contemporary technologies; while using the engineering design cycle to problem solve.  Through various course offerings students will develop an appreciation for the relationships among individuals, technology, and society.

COURSES IN DESIGN TECHNOLOGY

52540

Principles of Engineering I

0.5 Credit (Elective)

Grades 9-11

In this semester course, students will explore the four areas of technology education; communications, construction, manufacturing, and transportation and power technologies. During this activity-driven course, students will use the engineering design cycle to create solutions to a variety of STEAM assignments.All design challenges will help develop the students’ ability to analyze, use logical reasoning, and problem solve.  Concepts of power, force, velocity, acceleration, and speed will be covered.  Recent class projects have included building rockets, catapults, aerodynamic dragsters, simple machines and balsa bridges and creating promotional graphics, claymation videos.  Class will involve note taking, researching, quizzes and tests, but will focus heavily on hands-on projects with a strong emphasis on the importance of safety when working with tools. Students earning a 70% or higher in Principles of Engineering I may continue to Principles of Engineering II.  After successful completion of Principles of Engineering II, students will earn 1 science credit. This is offered every other year (2025-26, 2027-28) alternating with Principles of Engineering II (2026-27, 2028-29).

Primary Graduate Expectations:  Critical Thinking & Problem Solving; Effective Communication; Curiosity & Creativity.

Digital Photography/Imaging

Software training will focus on Adobe Illustrator and Adobe InDesign. Students will also learn the process of silk screen process printing. Students will participate in class critiques, demonstrations, and projects.  Class will involve notes, quizzes/tests, and critiques with heavy focus on hands-on projects. Knowledge of Adobe Photoshop is recommended, but not required.

521170

Mythology

0.5 Credit (English)

Grades 11-12

Pandora, Icarus, Achilles. Almost 3,000 years after their stories were first told, we still know these names. But while the Greeks created our best-preserved and most popular myths, every culture in human history has had its own mythology.  Many of these traditions have been lost or destroyed, but many others have survived, at least in part. This course is a comparative study of world mythology, with Greek mythology serving as our most in-depth case study and frame of reference.  We'll explore the complex patterns running through the world's myths: archetypes and enduring questions that give us glimpses of our collective psyche, our deepest hopes and fears and our most closely held truths. Along the way we'll engage with a broad array of texts, from the original primary sources of antiquity to the most modern reinterpretations and scholarship, including an ongoing focus on visual representations of the myths in art. Class discussion drives each lesson and is regularly assessed.  A project gives students the opportunity to engage more deeply with a myth of their choice, from the Greek or another mythical tradition.

Prerequisite: Successful completion of 10th grade English. 

Primary Graduate Expectations: Cross Cultural Understanding and Civic Engagement; Curiosity and Creativity

The mission of the Fine Arts Department is to develop a wide range of content knowledge and course specific skill sets in the visual arts and music. The overarching goal is to encourage a life-long openness to and appreciation for a range of human artistic expression.

Primary Graduation Expectations: Creativity & Curiosity and Critical Thinking & Problem Solving

To achieve the graduation expectations, a student must

  • perform or create with expression and accuracy within an artistic discipline
  • investigate the creative possibilities inherent in composing, arranging and improvising with insight, reason, technical proficiency

  • develop an informed personal aesthetic
  • improve upon product and performance through self-reflection and group critique

What’s the purpose of History and Social Studies?

The goals of this department are to:

  • Help students develop their reading and writing skills
  • Gain required knowledge to be more responsible and engaged members of our community, country and the world

In order to achieve our goals, we must study both the past and present.  

What are the required classes offered by the History/Social Studies Department?

BHS students are required to earn three credits in History/Social Studies. These three credits are:

  • Grade 9: Humanities/Civics (earned Honors available)
  • Grade 10: World History (College Prep or Honors)
  • Grade 11-12: American History (many options, including AP, and earned Honors available)

Does the History/Social Studies Department offer other classes outside of what is required?

YES! They offer much more!

  • Those are referred to as “Electives”, such as History Goes to the Movies or Psychology.
  • Some of the Advanced Placement classes (APs), like AP US Government and Politics, are also considered an elective.
  • Some of these electives are only available to seniors though.
  • If you are a student who loves history, then you are welcome to take more American History courses after you meet your requirements! Those classes would count as additional credits, showing as elective credit on your transcript.

BHS Graduation Requirement 

The Burlington High School graduate analyzes problems and the role of citizens using perspectives that reflect the differences among cultures and peoples by the application of knowledge and strategies learned from history and social studies.

To achieve the graduation requirement, a student must

  • use historical evidence to formulate positions, and viewpoints, and to understand current issues;
  • apply geographical principles;
  • explain democratic and constitutional principles and practices, compare them to other forms of government, and demonstrate the ability to take action within the political system;
  • explain how different cultures express, communicate, and instill their most important ideas;
  • analyze basic economic problems confronting individuals, nations, and the world.

LINK PROGRAM

The LINK program is a small school-based community for 11th and 12th-grade students serviced through an Individualized Education Plan (IEP) who require enhanced executive functioning, social-emotional, and academic support throughout their regularly scheduled school day.  LINK provides alternative pathways to graduation as well as proactive planning on post-graduate transitions into the community. In LINK, students have the opportunity to continue to practice the application of skills in core course assignments with individualized tutorial support within a structured small classroom environment. Entry into this program requires a referral from a school-based team at BHS.  

BRIDGING AT CHAMPLAIN COMMUNITY SERVICES

The Bridging Program is a dual-year commitment, nine (9) hours a week at Champlain Community Services that offers students the opportunity to participate with a cohort of peers from across other school districts in Chittenden County. They develop transitional life skills, including career exploration, community connections, independent living, and self advocacy skills in a supportive environment. Participants engage with community partners and businesses to learn about government, society, culture, environment, finances, and other topics related to student interests and life after high school. A certified teacher in a specific content area will serve as an advisor to the student and provide demonstrations of learning through an independent study model. A student can earn one (1.0) academic credit for each year of participation, as determined by a Special Education team, as well as potential elective credit(s). Entry into this program requires a referral from a school-based team at BHS.  

MATHEMATICS

The BHS Mathematics Program offers all students a preparation in the knowledge and skills necessary for competence in mathematics, as well as preparation for further training and study.  Courses aim to increase understanding of the basic concepts in mathematics; some will be taught using proficiency-based learning. Attention is given to providing a deeper knowledge of our number system, quantitative reasoning, study of the nature of proof and of the techniques of critical thinking, and knowledge of the contributions mathematics has made and is making to the progress of civilization.  An appreciation and enjoyment of mathematics as a way of thinking is sought.

BHS Graduation Requirement

The Burlington High School graduate uses a variety of mathematical methods and appropriate technology to solve problems and functions confidently in a mathematically sophisticated world.

To achieve the graduation requirement, a student must meet the following department graduation proficiencies:

  • Communicate their mathematics knowledge using appropriate vocabulary, proper context, and correct units when answering questions.
  • Solve questions using different methods utilizing multiple formulas and/or math tools.
  • Connect math concepts to real-world situations.  

Upon teacher recommendation and available space in classes, some students may double up with math courses in order to progress more quickly through the sequence of classes. Some students may also complete math requirements through VTVLC, EL, Instructional Services, and technical center classes.

Physical Education Pathways

Physical Education and Health courses are designed to provide students with the skills and knowledge necessary to lead a healthy and active lifestyle. Students are exposed to many different types of activities to encourage a lifelong pursuit of personal fitness. In Health, students learn a variety of topics included in the four main units: Mental Health, Nutrition, Substance Prevention and Abuse, and Sexuality and Relationships. Students will be provided current factual information to be able to make informed decisions regarding their own health. The Burlington High School graduate accepts responsibility for personal fitness by demonstrating the relationship between nutrition, physical activity, and personal lifestyles in determining health and life choices.

BHS Graduation Requirement

Physical Education – 1.5 Credits

  • At least 1.0 credit must be earned in BHS PE courses.
  • 0.5 credits may be earned in Interscholastic Sports in Grades 10-12; Fall only, Senior Year.
  • Only 0.5 credit can be earned in PE Capstone.
  • Health – 0.5 Credits

To achieve the graduation requirement in Physical Education, a student must demonstrate the following proficiencies set by the National PE Standards.

Standard 1: The physically literate individual demonstrates competency in a variety of motor skills and movement patterns.

Standard 2: The physically literate individual applies knowledge of concepts, principles, strategies and tactics related to movement and performance.

Standard 3: The physically literate individual demonstrates the knowledge and skills to achieve and maintain a health-enhancing level of physical activity and fitness.

Standard 4: The physically literate individual exhibits responsible personal and social behavior that respects self and others.

Standard 5: The physically literate individual recognizes the value of physical activity for health, enjoyment, challenge, self-expression and/or social interaction.

To achieve the graduation requirement in Health, a student must demonstrate the following proficiencies set by the National Education Health Standards.

Standard 1: Use functional health information to support health and well-being of self and others.

Standard 2: Analyze influences that affect health and well-being of self and others.

Standard 3: Access valid and reliable resources to support health and well-being of self and others.

Standard 4: Use interpersonal communication skills to support health and well-being of self and others.

Standard 5: Use a decision-making process to support health and well-being of self and others.

Standard 6: Use a goal-setting process to support health and well-being of self and others.

Standard 7: Demonstrate practices and behaviors to support health and well-being of self and others.

Standard 8: Advocate to promote health and well-being of self and others.

Science in Society

*Some BHS students take four or more science classes. It is strongly recommended that college-bound students complete ESS, Biology, Chemistry, and Physics.  

Science and technology offerings are designed to equip students with scientific understanding of the natural world through knowledge of the basic concepts of science, scientific and technological modes of inquiry, the nature of scientific and technological work, and the historical and social contexts of science and technology. Students will understand the basic concepts of the particular discipline they study, will be able to apply them to aspects of their own lives, and will be able to utilize them in combination with skills and knowledge obtained from other fields. The scientific and technological literacy students develop will enable them to make informed decisions in the home, community, and workplace.

BHS Graduation Requirement

The Burlington graduate understands and applies scientific and technological concepts to explain and demonstrate an understanding of the changing world.

Students will work toward proficiency in the following areas.

  1. Ask Questions: I can ask scientific questions and define problems.
  2. Developing and Using Models:  I develop and use models in order to construct explanations for scientific phenomena and design solutions for problems.
  3. Planning and Carrying Out Investigations: I can plan an investigation or test a design to produce evidence  to help me build and revise models, support explanations for phenomena, or test solutions to problems.
  4. Analyzing and Interpreting Data: I can analyze data in order to make valid and reliable scientific claims or determine an optimal design solution.
  5. Mathematics and Computational Thinking: I can use mathematical representations to describe and/or support claims and/or explanations.
  6. Constructing Explanations and Designing Solutions: I can construct and revise an explanation based on evidence or  design, evaluate, and/or refine a solution to a complex real-world problem.
  7. Engaging in Argument from Evidence: I can make a scientific argument that supports a claim using appropriate scientific evidence and reasoning.
  8. Obtaining, Evaluating, and Communicating Information: I can obtain, evaluate, and communicate scientific information.

Students should expect to read 150-250 pages per week and write one essay every two weeks.  This course provides preparation for the AP examination in English Literature and Composition and the AP examination in English Language and Composition.  Major works to be studied may include but are not limited to: Man's Search for Meaning, Slaughterhouse Five, Crime and Punishment, Portrait of the Artist as a Young Man, One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest, Life of Pi, Things Falls Apart, The Stranger, Waiting for Godot, To The Lighthouse, Siddhartha, and The Invisible Man. Students taking this course are able to demonstrate the ability to do college level work by analyzing literature in depth and writing an essay of analysis every two weeks.  Completion of a summer assignment is required for enrollment in this course.

High School Credits

Students will be awarded up to 3 (three) high school credits for each successful year in a BTC program. Please review individual program descriptions in the BTC Program of Studies for details. Please note that BTC core academic credits may be applied to Vermont high school graduation requirements, but the NCAA has not approved their use in the determination of collegiate athletic eligibility. Student-athletes who hope to compete in the future as an NCAA DI/DII athlete should work with their school counselor to assure necessary courses are still included in their BHS curriculum while they attend BTC.

Extended Learning Opportunities
Many BTC programs offer students Dual Enrollment/Fast Forward college credits for free within the program’s integrated curriculum. The opportunity to build a college transcript with transferable credits is valuable for students pursuing postsecondary education goals. BTC programs also offer pathways to earn professional certifications, industry-recognized credentials (IRCs), and/or licenses related to their technical fields as students build specialized and transferable skills. IRCs are one way a student can distinguish themselves from their peers in identifying a career pathway and potentially earn a higher wage. Students may also participate in work-based learning, an educational strategy that provides students with real-life work experiences that enhance and complement their academic instruction. For more information about BTC Work Based Learning experiences, go to the BTC WBL Website at https://sites.google.com/bsdvt.org/wbl-btc/wbl-btc.

Center for Technology, Essex (CTE)

The Center for Technology, Essex (CTE)  is a diverse community of learners that believes the dynamic integration of rigorous academic and technical instruction will prepare students for success in a wide array of careers and postsecondary endeavors. They strive to provide an equitable and safe learning environment and to ensure the personal, academic and technical growth of all students.  

CTE operates a full day, flexible block schedule to allow juniors and seniors to complete a technical program in one year. Students attend CTE daily from 10:05 am to 2:45 pm, with early release scheduled on Wednesdays at 2 pm. Each program offers two to three academic content proficiency areas (math, science, English, social studies, etc.) as well as a range of VT Agency of Education Transferable Skills toward high school graduation. In addition, many students take separate academic courses (e.g., algebra, chemistry) at CTE, Essex High School, or a local college to meet graduation or college entry requirements.

Many CTE programs qualify for dual enrollment credits that award eligible students college credit for their CTE program. These agreements include college transcripts and transferable credit. CTE students in all programs are also offered the opportunity to take college courses for free or at a reduced rate at area colleges.

6) Ability and willingness to follow safety instructions
